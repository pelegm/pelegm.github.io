Models

Some random graph models are defined not only by what is random, but also by what a player is allowed to choose. Here are three models I have introduced, proposed or helped develop.

Budget-constrained random graph process

One round

  1. An unseen edge is offered at random.
  2. Builder buys or rejects it, with no recall.
  3. Only purchased edges enter the graph and consume budget.

Introduced with Alan Frieze and Michael Krivelevich.

The edges of Kn arrive in a uniformly random order. Builder sees each edge once and tries to achieve a target property by time t, while purchasing at most b edges. The model separates the cost of observing random opportunities from the cost of accepting them.

Selected literature

  • Fast construction on a restricted budget Alan Frieze, Michael Krivelevich and Peleg Michaeli, RSA (2025) Introduces the model and studies connectivity, Hamiltonicity, matchings and small subgraphs.
  • Constructing Hamilton cycles and perfect matchings efficiently Michael Anastos, preprint (2022) Obtains asymptotically optimal time and budget for Hamilton cycles and perfect matchings.
  • Building graphs with high minimum degree on a budget Kyriakos Katsamaktsis and Shoham Letzter, EJC (2025) Develops more economical strategies for reaching prescribed minimum degree.
  • Graph factors and powers of Hamilton cycles in the budget-constrained random graph process Alberto Espuny Díaz, Frederik Garbe, Tássio Naia and Zak Smith, preprint (2025) Studies the time–budget trade-off for spanning structures.
  • Arithmetic progressions in a random set on a budget Jan Hladký, Daniel Iľkovič, Jared León and Xichao Shu, preprint (2026) Transfers the framework from graph edges to the first t elements of a uniformly random permutation of [n], so the offers are sampled without replacement.

Semi-random graph process

One round

  1. A vertex u is offered uniformly at random.
  2. Builder chooses a second vertex v online.
  3. The edge uv is added to the graph.

Proposed by me in 2016; formally introduced by Omri Ben-Eliezer, Dan Hefetz, Gal Kronenberg, Olaf Parczyk, Clara Shikhelman and Miloš Stojaković.

Randomness fixes one endpoint of every new edge; strategy controls the other. Builder aims to force a monotone graph property in as few rounds as possible. This small asymmetry produces a rich family of online construction problems.

Selected literature

  • Semi-random graph process Omri Ben-Eliezer, Dan Hefetz, Gal Kronenberg, Olaf Parczyk, Clara Shikhelman and Miloš Stojaković, RSA (2020) Formally introduces the process and establishes its first threshold results.
  • Very fast construction of bounded-degree spanning graphs via the semi-random graph process Omri Ben-Eliezer, Lior Gishboliner, Dan Hefetz and Michael Krivelevich, RSA (2020) Shows that every bounded-degree spanning graph can be built in linearly many rounds.
  • Perfect matchings in the semirandom graph process Pu Gao, Calum MacRury and Paweł Prałat, SIDMA (2022) Sharpens both sides of the time window for constructing a perfect matching.
  • Building Hamiltonian cycles in the semi-random graph process in less than 2n rounds Alan Frieze, Pu Gao, Calum MacRury, Paweł Prałat and Gregory Sorkin, EJC (2025) Constructs a Hamilton cycle in fewer than 1.817n rounds.

Random-walk trace model

One step

  1. The walk moves to a uniformly random neighbour.
  2. The edge just crossed is recorded.
  3. The trace is the subgraph formed by all recorded edges.

Developed in my work on finite random graphs, with Alan Frieze, Michael Krivelevich and Ron Peled.

A random walk on a host graph G leaves behind the subgraph consisting of the edges it has traversed. This trace is a dependent random graph: its local geometry comes from the walk, while its global structure also reflects the host.

Selected literature

  • Random walks on quasirandom graphs Ben Barber and Eoin Long, E-JC (2013) Shows that a long random walk on a dense quasirandom host leaves a quasirandom trace.
  • On the trace of random walks on random graphs Alan Frieze, Michael Krivelevich, Peleg Michaeli and Ron Peled, PLMS (2018) Studies Hamiltonicity, connectivity and hitting-time phenomena.
  • Small subgraphs in the trace of a random walk Michael Krivelevich and Peleg Michaeli, E-JC (2017) Determines thresholds for fixed subgraphs in traces on complete and random host graphs.
  • Note on the trace of random walks on pseudorandom graphs Yaobin Chen and Yiting Wang, preprint (2026) Answers two questions from the 2018 paper for pseudorandom and random regular hosts.
